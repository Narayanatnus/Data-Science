# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The one sentence already exists.** "Licensed, full-time early education for Naperville children aged 1 to 6, from a brand parents recognise." That is clear, and the Product lens rewards clarity.
- **The product is designed already.** A franchisor usually supplies the curriculum, the look of the center, the systems and the brand. Much of the experience is built before day one. (What TLE actually provides needs checking in its franchise disclosure document.)
- **The customer's moment is easy to describe.** Morning drop-off and evening pickup happen twice a day, about 250 days a year. When that handoff goes well, families stay for years. A business with a repeated, visible moment can be designed around it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The product is the staff, and the staff turn over.** In child care, the parent experiences the teacher, not the brand. Teacher turnover is a known industry-wide problem, and every departure breaks the relationship the parent pays for. The founder has not given a staffing plan, wage plan or director plan. In this business, a new teacher every few months is a broken product.
2. **The founder owns execution but not the design.** The franchisor controls curriculum, branding and most touchpoints. That leaves the founder few levers on the product: hiring, training, cleanliness, communication with parents, and how drop-off and pickup run. If the founder will not run the center day to day, even those levers go to a hired director. Day-to-day versus absentee is undecided, and that decision sets the quality of the experience.
3. **Seams the founder does not control.** The brand runs national marketing and enrollment tools; the center runs on-site care, local marketing and licensing. Parents see one product. If lead response, tours, billing or parent apps are split between the franchisor's systems and local staff, those handoffs are where the experience breaks. Whether $650,000 covers a build-out that matches the brand's standard is unknown, and a center that cuts corners on fit-out is likely to cut them elsewhere too (/founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What happens in the first five minutes of a parent's tour? And in the first five minutes of a two-year-old's first drop-off? Who is in the room?
- Will you run the center yourself? If not, who is the director, and what makes them stay?
- What will you pay lead teachers, and what is your plan for keeping them? This is a cost question (/founder-cfo) and a positioning question (/founder-competitors: what do nearby centers pay and charge?).
- Of all the touchpoints a parent sees, which ones does TLE own and which ones do you own? Where do they meet?
- What would you cut? (For example, launch with fewer age rooms or a smaller licensed capacity, and do those brilliantly.)
- Have you read the franchise disclosure document? What do the fee, royalties and total build-out cost come to (/founder-cfo)?
- Do Naperville parents want this at $1,300 to $1,400 a month, and what would make them switch from the center they use now (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors)?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A written operator decision: the founder runs the center, or a named director with child care experience runs it. Either way, a retention plan for teachers with pay set against local rates.
2. A map of every touchpoint from first ad to monthly invoice, each marked as owned by the franchisor or by the center, with the handoffs that could fail named.
3. The disclosure document reviewed, and /founder-cfo showing that $650,000 covers build-out, opening costs and a working-capital runway at brand standard, with no hidden cuts.
4. Local demand and pricing confirmed by /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer before a site is signed.
