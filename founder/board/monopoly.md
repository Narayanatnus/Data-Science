# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market that can be owned.** Child care is bought within a short drive of home or work, so the real market is a few square miles, not Naperville. A licensed center that fills one under-served pocket and keeps a waitlist has a local, defensible position. That is the "big share of a small market" this lens looks for.
- **Natural barriers to copycats.** Licensing, building and site requirements, staff-to-child ratios and long build-out times (general industry knowledge; Illinois specifics need verification) mean a rival cannot open next door in month six.
- **Sticky customers.** Families rarely move a settled child. Once a seat fills, it tends to stay filled for years, so each enrolled family is worth a lot over time.
- **Distribution is partly borrowed.** The franchise brand, website and lead flow give a route to the first families that a new independent center would not have.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No secret and no 10x advantage.** A franchise is, by design, a copy of a working model. The brand and the curriculum belong to the franchisor, and other franchised and independent centers offer something similar. If nearby centers already serve the same families at similar tuition, this is a fight over seats on price and staffing, which destroys margin. Who else is within reach of the same buyer is unknown (`/founder-competitors`).
2. **Distribution cost and fill speed.** Profit depends on how fast the center fills. How the first 100 families arrive, what each costs to win, and how many months it takes to reach break-even enrollment are all unknown (`/founder-consumer` for demand, `/founder-cfo` for cost per family and ramp).
3. **Durability depends on staff and the franchisor, not the founder.** Holding the position for ten years depends on keeping qualified teachers, the license record, and the franchisor's brand and terms. Staff turnover is a well-known problem in this industry. Royalties, fees, territory protection and renewal terms are not known (FDD review; `/founder-cfo`).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which few square miles of Naperville is this center for, and who else already serves families there, at what tuition, and with what waitlists? (`/founder-competitors`)
- Does the franchise give a protected territory? Could the franchisor or another franchisee open nearby?
- What do you know about this market that other operators do not: an unserved neighbourhood, an employer cluster, a site nobody else has?
- How do the first 100 families arrive, and what does each one cost? (`/founder-consumer`, `/founder-cfo`)
- Why now: is local demand growing faster than licensed seats?
- Who runs the center day to day, and what is the plan to keep teachers when the next center is hiring?
- Does $650,000 cover the franchise fee, build-out and the losses while it fills? (`/founder-cfo`, using the FDD)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. The competitor map shows a specific pocket of Naperville where demand outstrips licensed seats nearby, and the site is chosen there (`/founder-competitors`, `/founder-consumer`).
2. The FDD confirms a protected territory and royalty and fee terms that the CFO model can carry.
3. The CFO model shows the $650,000 covers build-out plus the ramp to break-even enrollment with a cash reserve, under a slow-fill scenario (`/founder-cfo`).
4. A named, experienced center director and a teacher-retention plan are in place before opening.
