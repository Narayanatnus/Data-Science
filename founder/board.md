# Board summary: The Learning Experience franchise, Naperville

## The vote
**3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass.** Average score **5.0 / 10**.

| Lens | Score | Vote |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Offers | 6 | FUND IF |
| Product | 5 | FUND IF |
| Monopoly | 4 | FUND IF |

Nobody passed, and nobody would fund it as pitched. The need is real and customers stay for years. The board's doubts are about the money, local competition and the staff. The board can't settle any of those three without more facts.

## Risks raised by more than one member
1. **$650,000 may not cover it (all three).** The franchise fee, royalties, marketing fund, build-out and working capital are all unknown. Child care centers can cost more than this to open, especially new builds. The money has to last until enrolment reaches break-even, including if it fills slowly. → `/founder-cfo`, using TLE's Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD).
2. **Staff are the product, and staff turn over (all three).** Parents judge a center by its teachers, not its brand. Teacher turnover is a well-known problem in this industry. No staffing plan, pay plan or director plan exists yet.
3. **Undifferentiated in a possibly crowded local market (all three).** A franchise curriculum is something other chains also offer. If nearby centers serve the same families at similar tuition, the fight is over price and staff. → `/founder-competitors`.
4. **The operator role is undecided (all three).** Whether you run the center or hire a director decides the quality parents see, and the Product lens argues it's the founder's main lever.
5. **Demand at $1,300–$1,400 a month and fill speed are unproven (Offers, Monopoly).** How the first families arrive, what each one costs to win, and how many months it takes to fill are unknown. → `/founder-consumer`, `/founder-cfo`.
6. **Franchise terms set the ceiling (Monopoly, Product).** Royalties, territory protection and renewal terms, plus who owns which parts of the parent's experience, all come from the FDD.

## Where the board disagreed
- **Offers (6)** is most optimistic. It sees urgent, recurring need, near-instant results and strong retention, and thinks a better offer can beat the commodity problem: extras, a guarantee and real scarcity.
- **Monopoly (4)** is most sceptical. A franchise is a copy by design, with no secret and no 10x advantage. It would fund only a site in a specific pocket of Naperville where demand outstrips nearby licensed seats, with a protected territory.
- **Product (5)** says the brand designs most of the experience, so the founder's product is execution: hiring, drop-off and pickup, and communication with parents. It suggests opening smaller and doing it brilliantly.

## Conditions checklist
Each condition says which step tests it.

- [ ] **FDD reviewed:** franchise fee, royalties, marketing fund, build-out, and territory protection and renewal terms (all three)
- [ ] **Cost model:** the $650,000 covers fees, build-out to brand standard, opening costs and the ramp to break-even enrolment, with a cash reserve, even if enrolment fills slowly (`/founder-cfo`; all three)
- [ ] **Break-even enrolment and age mix** worked out, given state staff-to-child ratios (`/founder-cfo`; Offers)
- [ ] **Protected territory** confirmed in the FDD (Monopoly)
- [ ] **Competitor map:** local tuition, capacity and waitlists, with a specific pocket where demand outstrips licensed seats, and the site chosen there (`/founder-competitors`; all three)
- [ ] **Demand confirmed** at $1,300–$1,400 a month before a site is signed (`/founder-consumer`, `/founder-pricing`; all three)
- [ ] **Operator decision in writing:** you run the center, or a named director with child care experience does (all three)
- [ ] **Teacher retention plan** with pay set against local rates (`/founder-ops`, `/founder-cfo`; all three)
- [ ] **Offer stack beyond the brand:** extras, a guarantee the numbers can afford, and real pre-opening scarcity such as founding-family rates and a waitlist (`/founder-offer`; Offers)
- [ ] **Touchpoint map:** every step from first ad to monthly invoice, marked franchisor-owned or center-owned, with the risky handoffs named (`/founder-ops`; Product)

## The strongest version the board can see
This is not quite what was pitched. It isn't "a TLE in Naperville". It's a TLE in one under-served pocket of Naperville, chosen because the competitor map shows waiting lists and too few licensed seats within a short drive. It's run day to day by you or a proven director, and it's known locally for keeping its teachers. It opens with a founding-family waitlist at full tuition, so it's partly full before it opens. It may launch with a smaller licensed capacity or fewer age rooms, sized so the $650,000 covers the build-out and a slow ramp with cash to spare. The brand gets families to the door; low teacher turnover and a smooth drop-off and pickup are what keep them for years. If the FDD numbers don't fit inside $650,000 with a reserve, the board would revisit the format or the site rather than stretch the money.
